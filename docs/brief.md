# Freelance Portfolio Website Brief

## Executive Summary

Dmitry Draganov's portfolio is a Russian-language acquisition website for freelance web-development work. It must help a prospective client answer four questions quickly: what Dmitry can solve, whether his work demonstrates sufficient technical quality, what a project may cost, and how to start a conversation.

The portfolio must position Dmitry through outcomes rather than seniority or a narrow job title. The core promise is: **build or improve web systems that solve a concrete client problem**. The strongest proof available at launch is the quality and breadth of two finished projects, supported by live demos, GitHub repositories, transparent process, indicative pricing, and a seven-day defect warranty.

## Audience and Need

The audience is intentionally broad: businesses, creators, startups, and individuals who need a website created, repaired, extended, or supported. Because the audience is not defined by one industry, the message must be organized around client situations:

- an existing site is broken, slow, outdated, or missing functionality;
- a new site must be implemented from requirements or a design;
- an existing product needs frontend, backend, CMS, integration, or maintenance work;
- the client wants a technically capable individual contributor who engages quickly and communicates directly.

The site should not attempt to prove expertise through an exhaustive technology list. It should translate capabilities into understandable services and use technology as supporting evidence.

## Positioning

**Core positioning:** Dmitry creates and improves web products that solve concrete business needs, from a focused fix to a complete site launch.

**Primary traits, in order:**

1. Quick to engage with the task.
2. Technically strong.
3. Reliable.

**Recommended hero message:**

> Превращаю задачи в работающие веб-решения
>
> Создаю сайты с нуля, исправляю ошибки и развиваю существующие проекты — внимательно к требованиям и деталям.

**Primary CTA:** `Обсудить задачу` opens Telegram directly. `Смотреть проекты` is the secondary hero action. Kwork is presented as an alternative transaction channel, not as the primary destination.

## Proof and Differentiation

The launch portfolio contains two dedicated case studies:

- **Weather Application:** a React and TypeScript weather product integrating four external services, interactive maps, cached data, localization, themes, and a custom Canvas chart.
- **Sound Engineer Website:** a responsive six-page website with a custom audio player, reusable modal and tab patterns, and a public demo.

These are personal projects. The site does not foreground their learning context, but must not imply a paid client engagement, testimonial, or measured commercial outcome. Proof comes from the implemented complexity, visible finish, live product, and source code.

Dmitry's working principles reinforce the proof: clarify requirements, agree scope before work, show intermediate progress, polish details, communicate honestly, and provide a limited post-delivery warranty.

## Services and Price Orientation

Prices are orientation points rather than packages:

- Fixes and small improvements — from 2,000 RUB.
- Landing page — from 15,000 RUB.
- Multi-page website — from 30,000 RUB.
- Custom functionality and integrations — assessed after scope clarification.
- Ongoing support and development — assessed by task volume.

Every estimate is individual. Outside Kwork, work starts with a 50% advance payment. New requirements introduced after scope approval are estimated separately.

## Experience Direction

The site should feel contemporary, technically confident, minimal, and memorable. The visual system combines strict typography, large-scale text, controlled experimental details, and purposeful scroll motion. A modular 3D object may assemble, break apart, and repair itself through the page to embody the create-and-fix proposition. It must never become decorative spectacle that obscures content or harms performance.

Dmitry's real portrait should be the default identity asset. AI may assist with background treatment or visual art direction, but the site should not replace an authentic portrait with a synthetic identity.

## MVP Scope

The first release includes:

- a responsive home page;
- separate pages for both projects;
- Russian copy for positioning, services, process, about, skills, pricing, and contact;
- Telegram, GitHub, email, and Kwork links;
- live-demo and GitHub links on each relevant case study;
- meaningful motion with a reduced-motion alternative;
- baseline technical SEO, sitemap, robots metadata, and Open Graph previews;
- Yandex Metrica page-view and conversion-event tracking;
- accessibility and performance guardrails.

The first release excludes a résumé download, education and English-language sections, testimonials, a contact form, blog, CMS, availability badge, and claims of commercial results that cannot be substantiated.

## Success Criteria

The website succeeds when a new visitor can understand the offer, inspect credible work, estimate whether the budget is plausible, and open a conversation without needing a résumé. At launch, success is measured by complete analytics instrumentation and the establishment of a 30-day baseline for project engagement and contact clicks. Conversion targets should be set only after a meaningful traffic sample exists.
